Teaching Time Without Losing General Retrieval: Adapting Multimodal Embeddings for Time-Sensitive Video Retrieval

Toya Oyama, Rainer Lienhart, Shin'ichi Satoh

查看原文摘要

In text-to-video retrieval, MLLM-based embedding models do well when the text describes the scene (general retrieval), but poorly when it describes actions and their direction (time-sensitive retrieval). TARA, a pioneering work, fine-tunes such a model on text triplets with temporal hard negatives; it improves time-sensitive retrieval but lowers general retrieval (mean R@1 over six benchmarks in our rerun on Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B: 58.4->44.8), and its released model keeps general retrieval only when ensembled with Qwen3-VL-Embedding. Our goal is strong general and time-sensitive retrieval with one model and one vector per clip. Three findings lead to three simple components. Keep loss: general retrieval follows how far the output embeddings drift from the base model, not how far the weights move; we therefore keep them near the base model, which keeps most general retrieval even without general-domain data. Pair loss: the text embeddings of an action description and its reversed-direction version are well separated, but the video embeddings of a clip and its reversed copy are close; we therefore widen the gap between the video embeddings of each clip and its reversed copy, in the direction of the difference between their two text embeddings. Middle-layer LoRA: frames are combined in the middle layers, and temporal information peaks in or just after them; we therefore train LoRA only on these layers, which nearly matches training all weights on most time-sensitive benchmarks with 0.15% of the parameters and keeps general retrieval better. General R@1 stays at 58.4, while R@1 with each clip's reversed copy in the gallery rises from 55.5 to 63.2; the gains carry over to unseen benchmarks and InternVL2-8B-RA. The keep loss is also a remedy for the TARA recipe (general R@1: 44.8->57.8), and ensembling TARA with Ours gives the highest scores on RTime and unseen CiA among the compared models.

FocusAnchor: Zero-Shot Metric Depth by Anchoring a Depth Foundation Prior to Focus Cues

Ashkan Ganj, Hang Su, Tian Guo

查看原文摘要

Existing depth-from-focus methods can struggle with weak focus cues, new cameras, or fewer focal slices. We present FocusAnchor, a recurrent model that uses focus-volume lookups to refine metric depth and repeatedly aligns a relative depth prior from a frozen foundation model to the current depth estimate. The aligned prior guides subsequent refinement in a feedback loop, carrying metric scale recovered from textured regions into regions where focus cues are weak while preserving scene structure. To support transfer across cameras, we synthesize training stacks with randomized camera parameters and focus distances. However, stack synthesis blurs existing image noise differently across slices, creating artificial depth cues even on textureless surfaces. We add independent per-slice noise to suppress these cues. We also introduce variable-stack training to improve robustness with fewer focal slices and support more practical mobile capture. Compared with FOSSA ViT-B, FocusAnchor reduces AbsRel by 36% on NYU Depth V2 and by 9% on DDFF-12 after fine-tuning. In zero-shot evaluation, FocusAnchor and its variable-stack variant, FocusAnchor-flex, achieve the two lowest AbsRel values on all five benchmarks. On ZEDD test, FocusAnchor achieves 9% lower AbsRel than the best competing method, FOSSA ViT-B, while FocusAnchor-flex achieves lower AbsRel with 40% fewer focal slices on ZEDD validation.

VidForce: Predicting Force-Aware Articulation by Observing Humans

Rafay Mohiuddin, Jiaqi Chen, Marc Pollefeys, Hermann Blum

查看原文摘要

People can anticipate how an object moves and the effort an interaction may require by watching others. How can embodied agents acquire this understanding from video? Force measurements provide direct sensory supervision, but require specialized equipment and remain limited in quantity. We introduce VidForce, which estimates an interaction trajectory together with a force profile from a third-person RGB video; 2D contact clicks and textual object-action prompts are optional. Our approach makes limited instrumented measurements useful for interpreting human interactions through learned physical prototypes as a prior. The prototypes share learned force levels and temporal profiles across interactions of comparable effort, with visual and semantic cues determining their contribution to each observation. Geometric and vision-language features adapt this prior to the observed interaction, while a joint conditional flow refines an initial tracked trajectory together with the force profile. Paired human and instrumented-gripper recordings provide measured force references for supervising human video, and a separately trained torque model demonstrates that the formulation extends to other sensory quantities. We evaluate trajectory recovery and force estimation through baseline comparisons and controlled ablations on held-out interactions from the Hoi! dataset. On this evaluation subset, VidForce achieves 6.6 cm ATE, peak-force MAE and RMSE of 4.81 N and 5.92 N, respectively, and force-profile RMSE of 5.52 N. On real-world videos, VidForce infers motion and force reliably and consistently despite the variability in human execution. We further deploy VidForce on a Spot robot, where trajectories and force profiles estimated from human demonstrations drive real-world articulated-object manipulation, and report success rates across repeated trials. Benchmarking Evidence Sufficiency in Vision-Language Models for Autonomous Driving

Nhat Ha Nguyen, Sudipta Chowdhury, Lokesh Das

查看原文摘要

Reliable driving VLMs must do more than answer correctly or localize relevant scene actors: they must determine whether the available visual evidence is sufficient to support an answer. Existing evaluations largely treat grounding and answerability as separate measurements, making it difficult to distinguish well-supported predictions from those localized to relevant but insufficient evidence. We introduce DriveAnswer, a benchmark of 20,151 questions across seven reasoning families, that operationalizes this distinction using matched strong- and weak-evidence configurations. Each pair preserves the underlying scene fact and semantic answer while changing whether the observations support answering, making abstention the appropriate response under weak evidence. On the six-family evaluation, the strongest zero-shot baseline reaches 67.31% strong-evidence accuracy, yet only 21.55% of matched questions satisfy both answer-abstain behavior and complete grounding. With controlled adaptation, Gemma-4-12B reaches 63.29% on this joint capability, nearly tripling its zero-shot performance. Qwen3-VL-8B exhibits the same broad improvement in evidence-sensitive answering and grounding. These results show that semantic correctness, evidence sufficiency, and grounding are distinct capabilities that should be evaluated jointly.

FedTumor-IoMT: Federated Learning-Aided Brain Tumor Diagnosis on Resource-Constrained Edge IoMT Devices

Pranshu Jena, Prajnyajit Mohanty, Umesh C. Pati

查看原文摘要

Joint brain tumor segmentation and classification models are computationally intensive, limiting their training on resource-constrained edge platforms and restricting privacy-preserving Federated Learning FL in the Internet of Medical Things IoMT. Existing approaches either perform both tasks on GPU-equipped workstations or support only classification on edge devices. To address this gap, this work proposes a lightweight multitask FL framework for joint segmentation and classification through local edge training. A shared pretrained LiteMedSAM encoder with TinyViT extracts features for a coordinate-attention-based segmentation decoder and a segmentation-guided classification head, eliminating a separate feature extractor. Selective encoder freezing retains 95\% trainable parameters while reducing on-device training costs. The proposed framework is experimentally validated on a laboratory testbed of five resource-constrained edge clients using 1,200 T1-weighted MRI samples. It achieves 94.20\% classification accuracy, 88.53\% DSC, 83.68\% IoU, and 99.87\% ROC-AUC. Its classification accuracy remains within 0.6\% of the GPU-trained baseline of 95.21\% across five FL rounds. The attention and feature refinement modules improve DSC and IoU by 3.82\% and 4.60\%, respectively, with 0.74 GFLOPs of additional computation. On edge hardware, the framework achieves 46.54 s inference latency, 52.8\% CPU utilization, and a peak temperature of 65$^\circ$C without thermal throttling. Its inference latency is observed to be 6.4--10.5 times lower than that of existing frameworks. These results demonstrate the feasibility of privacy-preserving joint brain tumor segmentation and classification on resource-constrained edge platforms.
