ICGF-CXRMate: Image-Conditioned Clinical Context Compression and Gated Fusion for Chest X-ray Report Generation

ICGF-CXRMate: Image-Conditioned Clinical Context Compression and Gated Fusion for Chest X-ray Report Generation

Vu Minh Tran, Thuc Huynh, Son Thanh Luu, Doanh C. Bui

核心贡献: 摘要生成失败
查看原文摘要

Auxiliary patient data can improve chest X-ray report generation, but directly concatenating heterogeneous clinical information with visual tokens produces long, variable-length decoder prompts and may expose the model to redundant or image-irrelevant context. We propose ICGF-CXRMate, an image-conditioned clinical context compression and gated fusion framework. Learnable queries are first conditioned on the current chest X-ray representations and then attend to the patient-data sequence, compressing it into a fixed number of clinical context tokens. Gated cross-attention selectively integrates these tokens into the visual stream, while the compressed tokens are retained in the decoder prompt as a direct clinical pathway. Experiments on linked MIMIC-CXR and MIMIC-IV-ED examinations show that ICGF-CXRMate improves report quality while reducing the average prompt length. In Stage~2, it improves RadGraph-F1 from 24.00 to 28.86, CXR-BERT similarity from 56.86 to 62.80, and BLEU-4 from 5.14 to 7.96 compared with CXRMate-ED, while reducing the average prompt length from 373.89 to 288.37 tokens, corresponding to a 22.9\% reduction. After self-critical sequence training, ICGF-CXRMate outperforms CXRMate-ED on six of eight report-quality metrics in Stage~3. Ablation studies confirm the complementary contributions of image-conditioned compression, gated fusion, and direct retention of the compressed clinical tokens.

When Annotation Patterns Are Learned: Skin-Tone Dependence in Dermatology Concepts

When Annotation Patterns Are Learned: Skin-Tone Dependence in Dermatology Concepts

Y. Li, M. Bigliardi-Qi, S. Bedros, P. Bigliardi, N. Papanikolopoulos

核心贡献: 摘要生成失败
查看原文摘要

Concept bottleneck models (CBMs) let clinicians inspect and correct skin disease diagnoses through clinical concepts, but only if the concept annotations are reliable. We therefore audit the reliability of SkinCon, a widely used dermatology concept dataset, across skin tones. Within the same disease and with near duplicates removed, erythema is annotated far less often on dark than on light skin, with an odds ratio of 0.17. In contrast, hyperpigmentation and hypopigmentation are annotated more often, with odds ratios of 6.2 and 4.5, respectively. We refer to this pattern as tone-dependent concept annotation. An evidential concept model trained on these annotations learns the same pattern and reproduces it on unseen images. Such a model may agree with potential erythema omissions rather than contradict them. Consistent with this, three label error detectors underperform a disease-only baseline at finding erythema that dermatologist captions mention but SkinCon labels as absent. The three detectors are based on confident learning, training dynamics, and the model's own evidential uncertainty. Concept annotations should thus be audited within each subgroup before explanations built on them can be trusted.

Energy Efficient Lung Tumor Segmentation with a modified CBAM ENet Skip Model

Sharan Thummagunti, Aayushi Padia, Abhiram Koppuravuri, Prabhu Prasad B M, G N Girish

查看原文摘要

Lung tumor segmentation plays a vital role in early detection and treatment planning of lung cancer. Although state-of-the-art deep learning models achieve high dice score coefficients (DSC), each forward pass requires computing large amounts of floating point operations, making them computationally expensive, requiring GPU acceleration, and having slow inference speed, limiting real-world clinical deployment. We propose a lightweight, energy-efficient model that leverages the ENet architecture for its real-time inference capability and low computational cost, enhanced with UNet-style skip connections between encoder and decoder stages for feature reuse and the lightweight Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) for improved feature representation. The model is trained on publicly available data from The Cancer Imaging Archive (TCIA) via the Medical Segmentation Decathlon Challenge (MSD) and evaluated on the Lung Image Database Consortium and Image Database Resource Initiative (LIDC-IDRI) dataset. Our model achieves a DSC of 0.90812 (threshold of 0.5) with its compact architecture of 430,052 parameters and 0.549 Giga Multiply-Accumulate operations (GMAC), making it up to 10 times faster, requires 79 times less GMAC than UNet, and has 22 times fewer parameters than SegNet. These characteristics make it well-suited for deployment in clinical settings where computational resources are limited and decrease the cost of utilizing such a system. Additionally, the explainable AI technique of Grad-CAM is used to provide visual interpretability of the model's predictions and to identify regions where segmentation performance requires improvement.

PathJev: Decision-Native Vision-Language Modelling for Histopathology

Cristian Tommasino, Antonio M. Rinaldi

Vision-language models used for decisions in histopathology rarely return calibrated probabilities over the candidates a user cares about. Decision-native models write a question and a candidate set chosen at run time into the input and return a normalised distribution from a small decision head in one forward pass. We build PathJev-P, a decision-native model for histopathology: MedGemma-1.5-4B with LoRA adapters and a decision head, trained on 40,000 patch states from three public collections whose expert labels are turned into typed questions with organ-specific candidate sets and a "none of these" option. In a comparison over 3 seeds with an analysis plan fixed before testing, on test sets without documented overlap with the training data of the backbone, PathJev-P is more accurate than a generative model trained with the same backbone, data and selection rule, the zero-shot pathology model CONCH and a general medical decision model (balanced accuracy 0.541 against 0.492, 0.345 and 0.213); its advantage over the generative model comes from two organs absent from training. The calibration advantage depends on how the generative model's probabilities are read out. With the summed candidate log-likelihood used in previous work, PathJev-P has the lower calibration error (ECE 0.204 against 0.279); with a length-normalised read-out of the same generative model, examined post hoc, the two have similar mean calibration error (0.201) and the generative model is better calibrated on the unseen organs, where PathJev-P remains over-confident. A supervised linear probe on a pathology foundation model is better calibrated in every setting. In a qualitative whole-slide demonstration, PathJev-P labels unsupervised tissue clusters with abstention and separates carcinoma from other tissue while running the model on a fraction of the tiles.

Evaluation of Uncertainty-Aware Multi-Software Ensembles for Hippocampal Segmentation

查看原文摘要

Accurate hippocampal segmentation can be a useful tool for diagnosing and monitoring neurological conditions such as Alzheimer's disease and epilepsy. While numerous automated segmentation methods exist, their clinical adoption remains limited. Reliable uncertainty assessment can enhance trust and facilitate clinical translation. This study evaluates ensembles of five heterogeneous hippocampal segmentation methods - InnerEye, ASHS, FastSurfer, HippoSeg, and FreeSurfer - across two dementia datasets and one epilepsy dataset. The sub-ensemble containing InnerEye, FastSurfer, and HippoSeg emerged as both accurate and efficient, highlighting the feasibility of balancing computational cost and performance. Additionally, ensemble-derived uncertainty quantification with sample variance, mutual information, and predictive entropy is shown to reduce inaccurate segmentations by flagging low-confidence cases, potentially providing a mechanism for automatically escalating ambiguous cases for expert assessment. Lastly, this study explores the downstream clinical utility of ensemble-derived uncertainty, finding a significant correlation with motion artefact severity and a modest contribution to Alzheimer's disease classification.
