Streaming across viewing conditions - The effect of viewing distance and display luminance on video quality

Dounia Hammou, Lukáš Krasula, Christos G. Bampis, Zhi Li, Rafał K. Mantiuk

查看原文摘要

The visibility of streamed video distortions is affected by the user's display and viewing conditions, such as their display's luminance or the viewing distance. Distortions become less visible at lower luminance levels or farther viewing distances. However, most image and video quality metrics assume fixed optimal display and viewing conditions, making them insensitive to the viewing conditions. In this work, we demonstrate how existing metrics can be adapted to account for both factors and compare such an adaptation with the metrics that natively model viewing conditions. First, we collected a new video quality dataset, HDR-VDC, which contains streaming distortions in 4K HDR videos. The dataset consists of 12 4K and 4 FHD HDR source video content, each distorted with AV1 encoding and Lanczos upscaling from a lower resolution. The quality loss due to the distortions was measured at two viewing distances, corresponding to 60 and 120 pixels per visual degree, and two display mean luminance levels, varying by a factor of 8 in terms of luminance. Next, we developed a pre-processing framework that lets us adapt many quality metrics so that they can evaluate content across the viewing distances and luminance levels. The framework employs a display model and perceptually uniform encoding, PU21, to account for the effect of display luminance, and perspective projection scaling (PPS) to account for the effect of viewing distance. We used the framework to compare the performance of 35 state-of-the-art metrics. This allowed us to identify a selection of existing quality metrics that can be effectively adapted to account for the viewing conditions.

Ocular Recognition: Interoperability, Multi-Resolution, and Spectrum Challenges

Fabian Stockhardt, Juan E. Tapia, Christoph Busch

查看原文摘要

Ocular recognition is typically developed under controlled acquisition conditions, limiting its use in semi-controlled and on-the-move scenarios. A key obstacle is the lack of representative data spanning real-world capture conditions. At a border checkpoint, for example, images may range from low-resolution visible-light periocular crops (RGB) to high-resolution near-infrared iris images (NIR). Using this heterogeneous evidence raises three questions: whether resolution-specific processing improves recognition, how to compare templates from different processing routes, and how to allocate training data across resolution specialists. We investigate resolution-specific processing and parameter sharing using three AdaFace-based architecture families: fixed-resolution specialists; Independent Backbones with Shared Heads (IBSH); and models with resolution-specific front-ends for 40x40, 80x80, and 160x160 inputs, followed by a shared identity trunk and embedding projection. We evaluate each architecture with route-specific training subsets and with a full-pool strategy, in which every specialist uses the complete selected training set. Results reveal a trade-off among same-resolution specialization, cross-resolution interoperability, and common-route re-extraction. Route-specific training produces distinct performance profiles across routes, whereas full-pool training makes route performance more uniform and primarily benefits the 160x160 route. Thus, adding training data does not improve all resolution routes equally.

AstroPIE-53: Towards Understanding Failure Modes of Human Pose Estimation in Space

Stephanie Käs, Marten Flaig, Lukas Woyke, Osmanalp Ömeroğlu, Felix Krueckel 等 (7 位作者)

查看原文摘要

Reliable monitoring of astronauts is essential for safety, situational awareness, and autonomous assistance in space missions. While modern computer vision enables accurate human detection and pose estimation on Earth, its robustness in space environments remains largely unquantified due to limited data availability. We present AstroPIE-53, a pilot dataset of 53 annotated astronaut videos collected from publicly available mission footage. To respect copyright restrictions, we release video identifiers, reconstruction scripts, and dense annotations rather than raw videos. The dataset includes bounding boxes, challenge classes, and human-annotated pose failure tags. We benchmark state-of-the-art person detection and pose estimation models under realistic zero-shot deployment conditions and perform the first systematic failure analysis for astronaut monitoring. Our results reveal substantial performance degradation caused by occlusions, unusual body orientations, low contrast, and microgravity motion patterns.

Radar Panoptic Transformer: Semantic-Guided Mask Queries for Dynamic Scene Understanding

Mayank Mayank, Bharanidhar Duraisamy, Rohit Mohan, Abhinav Valada

查看原文摘要

Automotive radar provides long-range sensing and direct Doppler velocity measurements, but its sparse and non-uniformly sampled point clouds pose significant challenges for panoptic segmentation. Dynamic objects often yield only a few detections amid a dominant static background, providing limiting the geometric cues for separating object instances. We propose \emph{Radar Panoptic Transformer (RPT)}, an end-to-end point-based network that incorporates radar-adaptive feature extraction with semantic-guided mask prediction. Its backbone aggregates pose-aligned temporal context through grouped vector attention and learns per-point neighborhood radii conditioned on range, azimuth, and radar cross section (RCS). Point-wise semantic predictions initialize queries at likely foreground detections and guide masked cross-attention through query-point class agreement, producing instance masks without geometric clustering. An auxiliary RCS-consistency loss matches instance-level statistics between predicted and annotated instances. RPT achieves state-of-the-art performance on RadarScenes with $71.6\%$ mIoU and $82.2\%$ PQ. We further evaluate RPT on MAN TruckScenes, where it outperforms the evaluated baselines in a long-range, full-surround radar setting with sensing ranges up to $230\,\mathrm{m}$. RPT achieves real-time end-to-end inference at $31$\,Hz on RadarScenes, exceeding the sensor frame rate.

DoF-Query Transformer for Adaptive Fusion of Egocentric Vision and Forearm HD-sEMG in Continuous Hand Kinematics

Jiahang Liu, Wenbo Zhang, Junsong Wang, Zhenzhi Ying, Siqi Hou 等 (7 位作者)

查看原文摘要

Continuous and accurate estimation of human hand kinematics is important not only for intuitive human-machine interaction, but also for capturing fine-grained human manipulation demonstrations that can provide action supervision for embodied-intelligence datasets and dexterous robot learning. However, individual sensing modalities may be insufficient for reliable estimation under varying operating conditions. We present a finger-unencumbered multimodal framework for continuous regression of 20 hand joint angles from egocentric RGB images and 128-channel forearm high-density surface electromyography (HD-sEMG). The framework combines a Vision Transformer-based visual encoder, a Transformer-based EMG encoder, and a degree of freedom (DoF) query Transformer fusion decoder. To account for the varying informativeness of visual and EMG cues across joint DoFs and input conditions, the decoder assigns a separate learnable query to each DoF, enabling DoF-specific and context-adaptive fusion of the two modalities. The framework was evaluated on synchronized multimodal data from 10 participants. Averaged across participants, the proposed fusion model achieved a mean coefficient of determination ($R^2$) of $0.88$ and a mean absolute error (MAE) of $2.80^\circ$, compared with $0.82$ and $3.21^\circ$ for the vision-only model and $0.68$ and $5.13^\circ$ for the EMG-only model. It also outperformed the evaluated representative unimodal baselines and alternative fusion designs. DoF-wise analyses showed larger fusion gains for visually ambiguous joints and viewing conditions, while synthetic visual occlusion demonstrated greater robustness than vision-only estimation. These results highlight the complementary value of egocentric vision and forearm HD-sEMG for capturing fine-grained human hand kinematics, with potential applications in human demonstration acquisition and dexterous robot learning.
