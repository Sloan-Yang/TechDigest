Stride: Scale-Aware Multi-Agent Evolutionary Refinement for Infographic Generation

Stride：面向信息图生成的尺度感知多智能体进化式精炼方法

Yan Zhou, Yang Yang

核心贡献: 提出了一种基于约束的多智能体框架Stride，通过尺度感知的路由策略（全局修订与局部修正分离）和单调接受机制，在任意冻结生成器上实现稳定迭代改进，显著提升了信息图生成质量，并超越了多个专有系统。
方法: Stride将约束评估（需求是否满足）与视觉定位（违规位置和方式）分离，并据此将全局修订任务分配给作曲家子智能体，局部修正任务分配给修图师子智能体。每个子智能体进行内部规划并生成多个候选方案，Stride仅在候选方案能提高验证器分数时接受，从而减少迭代中的性能回退。这种结合尺度感知路由、每步冗余和单调接受策略，确保了跨迭代的稳定改进。
关键发现: 在完全开源生成器-验证器系统下，Stride在BizGenEval上优于Nano-Banana-2.0和Seedream-5.0-Pro，在IGenBench上与GPT-Image-2.0持平并超越Seedream-5.0-Pro，在WISE上取得新的最优分数，同时在所有评估基准上显著优于现有智能体方法。
查看原文摘要

Infographic generation challenges text-to-image models because a single image must satisfy numerous visual, textual, and semantic constraints. Iterative agentic refinement with a multimodal verifier is a natural remedy, but existing methods face a scale mismatch: local edits cannot correct global composition errors, while full regeneration may destroy already-correct regions. We introduce Stride, a constraint-grounded multi-agent framework that treats refinement over any frozen generator as a history-aware evolution strategy. Stride separates constraint evaluation - whether each requirement is satisfied - from visual grounding - where and how it is violated. It then routes global revisions to a composer subagent and local corrections to a retoucher subagent. Each subagent conducts internal planning and generates multiple candidates, while Stride accepts a candidate only when it improves the verifier score, reducing regression across iterations. Because stochastic generators and imperfect verifiers make any single step unreliable, it is this combination of scale-aware routing, per-step redundancy, and monotonic acceptance that yields stable improvement across iterations. With fully open-weight generator-verifier systems, Stride outperforms proprietary systems including Nano-Banana-2.0 and Seedream-5.0-Pro on BizGenEval, matches GPT-Image-2.0 and surpasses Seedream-5.0-Pro on IGenBench, and achieves a new state-of-the-art score on WISE, while substantially outperforming existing agentic methods across all evaluated benchmarks.

InstantFusion: A Shared Latent Interface for Heterogeneous Image Generators

即时融合：异构图像生成器的共享潜在接口

Xuanlang Dai, Jiazi Bu, Yujie Zhou, Yuhong Liu, Beichen Zhang 等 (8 位作者)

核心贡献: 提出一种轻量级共享潜在接口（InstantFusion），无需为每对模型单独训练映射，即可实现不同架构和表示的图像生成器之间的无缝状态转换，支持加速、多偏好组合、跨架构策略蒸馏和跨模型组合等多种应用。
方法: 该方法引入模型特定的潜在自编码器（LAEs），将不同生成器的中间状态投影到共享潜在空间，并解码为目标生成器的潜在空间。共享空间通过两个互补目标约束：对齐来自同一图像在匹配噪声水平下的中间状态，以及同步对应的局部去噪速度。训练完成后，InstantFusion允许在冻结的生成器之间无缝切换去噪过程，无需为每对模型设计专用映射。
关键发现: 实验表明，独立训练的生成器具有相似的潜在空间组织，直接传递中间状态可保留粗略视觉内容；InstantFusion在加速、多偏好组合、跨架构策略蒸馏和跨模型组合等任务中均达到竞争性性能，验证了其通用性和有效性。
查看原文摘要

Diffusion/flow models have emerged as the de facto paradigms for image generation, giving rise to a diverse family of modern generators with increasingly heterogeneous architectures and representations. Despite their remarkable generation capabilities, the cross-model alignment of latent representations and the consistency of their underlying denoising dynamics remain underexplored. In this work, we uncover an intriguing cross-model compatibility: independently trained generators exhibit similar latent spatial organization, while directly handing off intermediate states between unaligned models can still preserve coarse visual content. Motivated by these observations, we propose InstantFusion, a lightweight shared latent interface that connects the denoising trajectories of heterogeneous image generators. Specifically, to enable flexible cross-model state translation, model-specific Latent AutoEncoders (LAEs) are introduced to project intermediate states from different generators into a shared latent space and decode them into the latent space of a target generator. This shared space is constrained by two complementary objectives: aligning intermediate states derived from the same image at matched noise levels and synchronizing their corresponding local denoising velocities. Once trained, InstantFusion allows denoising to seamlessly transition across frozen generators without requiring a dedicated mapping for every model pair. The versatility of InstantFusion is demonstrated across acceleration, multi-preference composition, cross-architecture on-policy distillation, and cross-model composition, achieving competitive performance across these diverse settings.

Images Talk Back: Guiding MMDiTs by Suppressing Image-to-Text Feedback

Images Talk Back: Guiding MMDiTs by Suppressing Image-to-Text Feedback

Yucheng Yang, Xiaoxiao Sun, Shusheng Yang, Jiakang Chen, Serena Yeung-Levy

核心贡献: 摘要生成失败
查看原文摘要

Multimodal diffusion transformers (MMDiTs) allow the evolving image to update internal text representations through joint attention. These representations then guide subsequent image updates, raising a question: does this feedback help correct deviations from the prompt, or preserve them? We find that image-to-text (I2T) feedback can preserve the objects and attributes already represented in the evolving image, including errors. Transplanting I2T messages can rescue failures or disrupt successes. At matched intervention strengths, suppressing the influence of I2T feedback improves generation, whereas opposite controls degrade it. These findings motivate Semantic Feedback Guidance (SFG), which suppresses the influence of I2T feedback by guiding away from an auxiliary branch that strengthens this feedback and weakens text-to-image conditioning. The in-place variant, iSFG, directly suppresses I2T feedback and strengthens text-to-image conditioning, requiring no extra model evaluations. Temporal interventions motivate running the SFG branch during the first 20% of image sampling steps, requiring about $1.2\times$ the base NFE without classifier-free guidance (CFG). Experiments across three image-generation backbones show gains in alignment and preference-model scores. On FLUX, adding SFG to CFG raises GenEval from $0.6661$ to $0.7479$; on SD3.5M, iSFG adds $+1.33$ HPSv2.1 over CFG at zero extra NFE. The control also transfers to text-to-video and image-to-video generation, improving VBench scores. Code is available at https://github.com/yyc1215lll-creator/SFG.

TextVBench: Evaluating Text Rendering and Preservation in Video Generation

TextVBench：评估视频生成中的文本渲染与保持

Ruiqiang Zhang, Hengyi Wang, Chang Liu, Guanjie Wang, Zehua Ma 等 (6 位作者)

核心贡献: 提出了一个名为TextVBench的基准和评估框架，将视频生成中的视觉文本评估形式化为多目标、多实例的时空对应问题，以解决现有方法无法准确衡量文本渲染错误和跨帧不一致的问题。
方法: TextVBench包含两个互补的评估轨道：T2V（文本到视频）和Ref2V（参考图像到视频），分别衡量提示指定文本的创建与保持以及参考图像中文本的保持。评估采用目标条件候选选择而非无约束的VLM转录，支持一个目标映射到多个OCR区域，并分离帧级空间保真度和跨帧时间保真度。最终使用长度加权调和分数汇总这些因素，并通过额外文本诊断揭示单一OCR分数掩盖的失败模式。基准围绕受控的文本、布局、载体和运动属性组织，使聚合分数可追溯到具体能力限制。
关键发现: 在评估的模型运行中，MiniMax-H3在T2V轨道上达到68.32的TextVScore，而最强的Ref2V分数为56.51；属性切片显示，随着同时参考文本区域数量的增加，性能显著下降。
查看原文摘要

Modern video generators can synthesize convincing scenes while rendering a few characters incorrectly, omitting requested text, or changing it across frames. Measuring these failures is harder than applying OCR independently to each frame: when a scene contains multiple text targets and multiple noisy OCR regions, the evaluator must first determine which observation belongs to which intended text. We introduce TextVBench, a benchmark and evaluation framework that formulates visual-text evaluation as a multi-target, multi-instance spatiotemporal correspondence problem. TextVBench contains two complementary tracks: TextVBench-T2V measures the creation and maintenance of prompt-specified text, while TextVBench-Ref2V measures preservation of text from reference images. Both tracks use target-conditioned candidate selection rather than unconstrained VLM transcription, support one target mapping to multiple OCR regions, and separate frame-level spatial fidelity from cross-frame temporal fidelity. A length-weighted harmonic score summarizes these factors, while extra-text diagnostics expose failure modes hidden by a single OCR score. The benchmark is organized around controlled text, layout, carrier, and motion attributes so that aggregate scores can be traced back to specific capability limits. Across the evaluated model runs, MiniMax-H3 reaches a T2V TextVScore of 68.32, while the strongest Ref2V score is 56.51; attribute slices reveal a pronounced drop as the number of simultaneous reference text regions increases.

ThinkDrag: Semantic Drag-Based Image Editing with Visual Reasoning

ThinkDrag：基于视觉推理的语义拖拽图像编辑

Armando Fortes, Tianyi Wei, Chindanai Trakantannarong, Shangchen Zhou, Xingang Pan

核心贡献: 提出一个统一的多模态框架ThinkDrag，将拖拽编辑从单纯的几何位移提升到语义层面，通过显式推理轨迹和拖拽端点标记实现更符合内容理解的图像编辑，并构建了监督数据集和基准DragBench++。
方法: ThinkDrag结合多模态输入（图像、点约束和可选文本），训练模型将点约束映射到对象或场景级别的语义变换，并生成显式推理轨迹以解释编辑意图。通过分析交叉图像注意力引入拖拽端点标记，提供点约束在图像空间中的对应信号，增强编辑的准确性。模型采用监督学习，使用包含推理轨迹的语义拖拽变换数据集进行训练。
关键发现: 在DragBench++基准上，ThinkDrag达到最先进性能，生成质量和合理性显著提升，同时保持与现有方法相当的点跟随精度，尤其在模糊或语义复杂的拖拽场景中表现优越。
查看原文摘要

Drag-based image editing provides an intuitive interface for spatially controlled image manipulation by allowing users to specify handle and target points. Existing methods have made substantial progress through optimization-based, one-step, and feed-forward formulations, but they often interpret drag instructions as geometric displacements. This limits their effectiveness when the desired edit depends on interpreting image content and inferring a plausible semantic transformation from sparse point constraints. We introduce ThinkDrag, a unified multimodal framework for drag-based image editing with visual reasoning. ThinkDrag is trained to associate point constraints with meaningful object- and scene-level transformations, and can optionally produce an explicit reasoning trace that interprets the intended edit before image generation. This reasoning trace improves interpretability, particularly for ambiguous drags. Drag Endpoint Markers, motivated by an analysis of cross-image attention, provide an explicit correspondence signal that grounds each drag in image space. To support this framework, we construct a supervised dataset of semantic drag transformations paired with reasoning traces and introduce DragBench++, a benchmark targeting challenging drag-based editing scenarios with reference edit solutions. Experiments show that ThinkDrag achieves state-of-the-art performance, improving generation quality and plausibility while maintaining competitive point-following precision.
