RoboBuilder: Agentic Robot Co-Design with Real-world Video Feedback in the Loop

Ghadi Nehme, Dahyun Nam, Akshay Govind Srinivasan, Faez Ahmed

查看原文摘要

Can AI agents design robots that work in the real world? We introduce RoboBuilder, a benchmark in which coding agents co-design a robot's hardware and program from standard parts, motors and a custom 3D-printed part, and every design that passes in simulation is built and tested on a physical course. Simulation alone is not enough: under public part data, none of 15 initial passing designs crossed the real course, so an agent learns the simulator from measurements it requests and videos of real runs. Across four frontier coding agents, ten obstacles at three difficulty levels, 16,427 simulations of 12,774 distinct robots and 135 physical robots built in an estimated 240 hours, agents solve nearly all easy obstacles and invent mechanisms our search baselines do not reach, but falter at harder levels, when one robot must cross every obstacle, and in the real world, where 66% of simulated passes hold across all levels. Closing the loop helps: shown a video of its real failure, an agent redesigns the robot, sometimes correcting its simulator, and removes 72% of level-1 and level-2 failures. RoboBuilder measures progress toward agents that engineer real machines. Project Page: https://ghadinehme.com/robobuilder.github.io/

ReflectScene:MLLM-Guided Closed-Loop Diffusion for 3D Indoor Scene Generation

Jiawei Shen, Jie Feng, Junpeng Zhang, Guanbin Li, Wanyi Chen 等 (7 位作者)

查看原文摘要

Text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation requires layouts satisfying geometric, functional, and semantic constraints. However, existing diffusion methods rely primarily on static textual conditions, limiting their ability to assess evolving scenes and translate semantic discrepancies into executable object-level corrections. MLLM-based post-hoc refinement provides semantic feedback but remains decoupled from denoising, potentially disrupting scene coherence.We propose ReflectScene, an MLLM-guided closed-loop diffusion framework. First, we introduce Hierarchical Prior-Grounded Scene Understanding that aligns structured layouts with multi-view visual evidence, to interpret object-specific context and scene global semantics via a frozen multi-modal large language model (MLLM). Second, we propose MLLM-Guided Semantic Reward Evaluation to distill MLLM's diagnoses and assessments on possible candidate corrections into a lightweight Reward Predictor, enabling correction decision making with reduced computation burden. Finally, Decision-Conditioned Trajectory-Consistent Diffusion is proposed to integrate selected corrections through transient conditioning and persistent clean-estimate projection, correcting object layout in the generated scene while preserving non-target arrangements throughout denoising.Experiments on 3D-FRONT demonstrate improved functional consistency and semantic alignment while maintaining competitive generation quality and diversity.

TOGBench: Benchmarking and Improving Tiny-Object Generation Fidelity in Aerial Imagery

Chanhwi Jeong, Dongmin You, Seungwook Kim

查看原文摘要

Advances in deep generative models now enable the synthesis of realistic imagery. However, aerial imagery is dominated by tiny objects, such as vehicles, ships, and aircraft, which occupy only a few pixels owing to the overhead, wide-area nature of aerial imaging. Existing generative models struggle substantially more with tiny objects than with larger ones. This limitation has been overlooked, as prior work on generative evaluation emphasizes prompt following or human preference rather than whether small objects are rendered faithfully. To address this gap, we introduce TOGBench, a quantitative, object-level benchmark for evaluating tiny-object generation fidelity in aerial imagery. We further propose two simple train-/inference-time techniques that improve the ability of layout-to-image generators to render small objects, raising their performance on TOGBench.

Learning Martial Arts Motion Skills for Animating Opponents in VR Sparring Games

Jiqing Wen, Qixuan Shi, Robert LiKamWa, Pavan Turaga

查看原文摘要

Animating virtual opponents is an important yet challenging task in game development. In martial arts games, opponent motions must not only be physically plausible but also exhibit appropriate technique, timing, transitions, and target-directed intent. Traditional game animations often rely on handcrafted clips, state machine transitions, and procedural adjustments, which can be labor-intensive and may limit the behavioral diversity of non-player characters. This paper presents a martial arts motion skills learning framework for character animation in virtual sparring games. Inspired by Tang Soo Do, we collected a martial arts motion capture dataset containing isolated attacking and defending techniques, technique combinations and transitions, and contextual sparring variations. We first pretrained a reusable repertoire of martial arts motion skills from the captured data using reinforcement learning (RL) with adversarial style rewards, and then adapted these skills with task-specific rewards for target-directed striking and threat-aware evasion. We evaluated the motions generated by the learned policy through a user study, in which participants assessed their physical plausibility, smoothness, and martial arts form. To demonstrate their applicability to interactive gameplay, we developed a VR sparring prototype for Meta Quest headsets. The prototype combines policy-generated motion clips with lightweight state-based control to support interactive opponent behavior. Together, these findings support the feasibility of using a learned martial arts skill repertoire as a motion foundation for animating opponents in VR sparring games.

Mapping the Design Space of Feature Sampling for Optical Flow

Liping Zhang

查看原文摘要

The computation of correlation features is crucial for optical flow estimation. Existing methods (e.g., RAFT) perform fixed-radius lookups on a cost volume, yielding a set of correlation features for each position; alternatively, WAFT performs single-point feature indexing, yielding one correlation feature vector per position. None of them designs a suitable correlation feature sampling scheme for each position according to regional characteristics. Our insight is that not every position requires the same sampling scheme. Applying an identical sampling scheme to every position is either redundant or insufficient and ignores the specific characteristics of the corresponding region. This paper rethinks the design of feature sampling and investigates the following question: should the support of feature sampling---i.e., the extent and directions over which evidence is aggregated---be learned separately for each query position? To study this question, we propose SAGE (Sampling with Anisotropic Gaussian Expectation). This method replaces single-point sampling with a per-pixel anisotropic Gaussian expectation on a fixed candidate grid centered on the current optical flow estimate. A positive-definite precision matrix controls the scale, anisotropy, and orientation of the sampling support, with single-point sampling included as its limiting case. Consequently, the sampling support becomes an explicit and analyzable design variable and provides a principled way to analyze how directional feature aggregation affects correspondence learning. Extensive experiments show that SAGE, while adding only 1.5% multiply-accumulate operations (MACs), effectively improves zero-shot cross-dataset generalization. When trained only on FlyingChairs and FlyingThings, SAGE reduces the end-point error (EPE) by 24.2% and 16.4% on Sintel clean and final, and reduces KITTI F1-epe and F1-all by 7.2% and 11.6%.
