Estimating Image Creativity

Nisan Chhetri, Christopher G. Healey, Sina Baghbanijam

查看原文摘要

What makes an image creative? Although this may seem like a highly subjective decision, research in psychology suggests that creativity may be based on a set of fundamental creativity dimensions such as novelty, surprise, and arousal. A deeper understanding of image creativity can benefit both research and practical applications. Exploring how human viewers use creativity dimensions to evaluate image creativity may allow us to predict a target image's human-rated creativity automatically. We present a novel framework to estimate the perceived creativity of an image by: (1) predicting the strengths of an image's novelty, surprise, and arousal creativity dimensions, and (2) combining the creativity dimension strengths to estimate an image creativity score. Controlled human-subject experiments were conducted on Amazon Mechanical Turk to collect data to train supervised machine learning models to estimate an image's strengths on the three creativity dimensions. A final machine learning model takes the predicted dimension strengths as input to produce an estimated image creativity score. Results showed that our estimated creativity scores are statistically equivalent to human ratings of creativity for the images we tested. The novel contributions of our research are: (1) identification and validation of three psychological creativity dimensions used to estimate image creativity; (2) supervised machine learning models to automatically estimate novelty, surprise, and arousal strengths for an input image; (3) a supervised machine learning model to predict a creativity score from novelty, surprise, and arousal strengths for an input image, and (4) experimental methods to confirm the accuracy, advantages, and limitations of our approach.

AROMA: Adaptive Representation-Oriented Multimodal Actuation for Vision-Language Model Control

Zhaoning Zhang

查看原文摘要

Mechanistic localization does not by itself yield usable control: causal sensitivity may occupy little activation energy, an accurate local direction may fail at finite scale, and a reachable numeral target may be wrong. We introduce AROMA (Adaptive Representation-Oriented Multimodal Actuation), a frozen-VLM control framework separating where to intervene, which geometry and gain to use, how to reach a target, which target to pursue, and when to act. In Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct, four dimensions of a causally validated cross-attention head explain 89.82% of cardinality sensitivity but only 9.52% of median activation energy; magnitude-normalized causal subspace actuation converts this mismatch into finite control, improving exact-count accuracy on untouched procedural data by +8.00 pp. Iterative Minimal-Effort Actuation (MEA) reaches 81.25% of oracle targets versus 15.18% for a frozen direction under matched step budgets. With target and gate frozen before evaluation, targeted MEA improves the previous frozen policy from 34.444% to 38.730% on a new natural Stage-2 cohort (N=630; paired p=4.63e-7) and yields 65.741% versus 65.153% over all 7,992 examples while editing only 63 inputs. Prospective routing, replication across heads and models, and comparisons with attribution-steering baselines further characterize each stage, separating localization, actuation, reachability, target quality, and selective deployment as distinct control problems.

Back to the Question: Tracing Visual Distraction Through Text Tokens

Marie Amale Huynh, Regev Cohen, Serena Yeung-Levy, Sivan Doveh

查看原文摘要

The visual world is cluttered: robust vision-language reasoning requires ignoring irrelevant information. Yet vision-language models (VLMs) can suffer substantial accuracy losses when answering questions about a target image with distractors present. Existing mitigations focus on selecting relevant images or limiting information exchange between images. Through a systematic analysis of three open 7-8B VLMs on five perception benchmarks adapted to include distractor images, we show that distractors reduce accuracy even when models correctly identify the relevant image, suggesting that selection alone is not enough. To understand the mechanism behind this interference, we combine attention analysis with targeted knockouts in Qwen2.5-VL and identify middle-layer distractor-to-text connections as a pathway through which irrelevant visual information affects reasoning. We introduce answer consistency regularization, which encourages consistent answer distributions with and without distractors. Without directly constraining attention, this objective reduces middle-layer attention from question tokens to distractor images, consistent with the pathway our analysis implicates. With five distractors, our approach improves circular accuracy by an average of 16 percentage points over the Qwen2.5-VL baseline across the five benchmarks while preserving single-image performance.

Federated Class Incremental Learning: A Pseudo Feature Based Approach Without Exemplars

Min Kyoon Yoo, Yu Rang Park

查看原文摘要

Federated learning often assumes that data is fixed in advance, which is unrealistic in many real-world scenarios where new data continuously arrives, causing catastrophic forgetting. To address this challenge, we propose FCLPF (Federated Class Incremental Learning with Pseudo Features), a method that uses pseudo features generated from prototypes to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. Our approach reduces communication costs and improves efficiency by eliminating the need for past data and avoiding computationally heavy models like GANs. Experimental results on CIFAR-100 show that FCLPF achieves an average accuracy of 51.87% and an average forgetting of 9.62%, significantly outperforming existing baselines with an average accuracy of 47.72% and forgetting of 20.46%. On TinyImageNet, FCLPF achieves 37.56% accuracy and 3.14% forgetting, compared to the baselines' 27.69% accuracy and 24.46% forgetting, demonstrating the superior performance of FCLPF.

Validation of a Facial-Video Digital Hydration Biomarker Score for Tracking Hydration State Transitions in a Real-World Fasting Model

Yudara Kularathne, D. M. J. Prathapa, Thanveer Ahamed, Janindu Athukorala, Thisara Perera

查看原文摘要

Hydration influences physiology and daily function, yet repeated practical measurement in everyday settings remains limited. We present initial real-world evidence that a facial-video lifestyle digital biomarker score can track the direction of hydration state transitions. Using Ramadan fasting as a model of prolonged fluid abstinence, we assessed whether the score tracks the transition between the fasting (pre-drink) state and the rehydrated (post-drink) state across three validation layers. In the primary cohort (104 participants, 208 videos), the score increased from a mean of 36.6 in the fasting (pre-drink) state to 70.6 in the rehydrated (post-drink) state (change = +33.9, 95% CI [+30.7, +37.1], p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 2.03), with 102 of 104 participants (98.1%) showing an upward shift. In a nested body-weight subgroup (20 participants), body weight increased by +0.74 kg after fluid intake (p < 0.001), confirming fluid uptake and providing supportive physical anchoring. In a nested serum osmolality subgroup (10 participants), osmolality decreased by -5.1 mOsm/kg (p < 0.001), with all post-drink values within the normal clinical range, providing exploratory biological support. An exploratory secondary comparison of water versus electrolyte rehydration showed no statistically significant difference in short-term score change (p = 0.247). This study does not establish a clinical diagnostic test for dehydration. Rather, these findings support the score as a lifestyle digital biomarker for tracking the direction of hydration state change in real-world settings.
